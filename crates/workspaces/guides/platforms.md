# Platforms and formats

Each format in the brief is a size this video is delivered in. The platform, when the creator named one, is where it will be posted.

- Frame for every format. In a vertical (9:16) format, keep faces, text and key visuals in the upper and middle part of the frame.
- On vertical feeds (YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram Reels) the app draws the account name, caption and buttons over the bottom of the video and down its right edge, and often a little at the top. Keep text and anything important out of the bottom quarter, the top eighth and away from the right edge. The safe margin animations are checked against is the minimum for every format; vertical feeds need this extra room.
- Keep the essentials inside the central square of a landscape design so square and portrait formats keep them. A vertical version is restacked, not just cropped: side-by-side elements go one above the other, the focal element gets bigger and holds get a little shorter.
- Feeds decide in the first seconds. Open on the promise, the result or the most striking moment, not on a logo, greeting or slow build-up.
- Feeds often start videos muted. Let on-screen text carry the message, and keep spoken points visible on screen.
- In feeds, 4:5 tends to take more of the screen than square.
- YouTube Shorts can be up to three minutes long. Instagram may not recommend Reels longer than three minutes to people who do not follow the account, so keep a Reel meant to reach new people under three minutes.
- On YouTube in landscape (16:9), viewers chose to watch: build the argument, mark chapters with clear title cards, and leave room to breathe. YouTube end screens can cover the last 5 to 20 seconds of a video that is at least 25 seconds long; if the creator uses them, keep that stretch visually calm.
- Square and landscape videos in professional feeds such as LinkedIn play small: large, high-contrast text and a clear first frame matter most.
- A shorter version is a re-edit, not a trim: lead with the strongest hook, keep the one message and the call to action, and rework the transitions so it flows.
