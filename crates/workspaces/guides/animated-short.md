# Animated shorts, stories and moods

- Let the topic and the people watching decide the look and the sound, not earlier videos: unrelated topics deserve different looks.
- Stories: a title beat of two to four seconds, a middle in which something changes, and a closing moment that settles and stays on screen. Each beat should happen because of the one before it ("the alarm never rings, so she runs for the train"), not merely after it. A reveal works best as two scenes: what happened, then the reaction to it.
- Moods and poems: no plot, one feeling and two or three images that turn into each other, six to ten seconds each, with slow fades and little text.
- Loops: the last frame matches the first. Build every movement from cycles so the seam disappears.
- Vary the framing as a film does: a wide scene to set the place, a medium one for the action, a close one on a detail, and at least one slow push in or pull out.
- Keep every scene alive, even in a long hold: drifting light, breathing, smoke or a slow camera move.
- Keep continuity: what is held stays held, the hour and the lighting stay the same, and anything travelling to the right is still travelling that way after a cut. Let an object cross each cut with the viewer, or move in on a small detail that opens the following scene.
- Stage things believably: objects rest on surfaces and figures stand on the ground. Nothing floats unless that is the joke.
- Draw the comparison, don't caption it. When something on screen represents a concept, put that concept visibly on it rather than writing a label beside it.
- Illustrated pieces: make each element its own image, one subject on a plain background with no words in it, and move each one whole from its natural pivot. Add every word as text. Let each entrance suit its idea (splitting, flooding, counting or snapping into place) rather than making everything grow.
- Endings: wait until everything has stopped moving, pause, and only then bring in the closing line.
- Give every visible action that should make a sound its sound, and leave a moment of quiet before the payoff.

Checks: each scene can be read from a thumbnail; nothing floats or collides with text; the closing line appears after the motion settles; a loop's ends match.
