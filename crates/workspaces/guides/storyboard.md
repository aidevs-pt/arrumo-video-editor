# Storyboarding a video without footage

- Each scene carries one beat of the script. If a beat needs two ideas, make two scenes.
- A viewer with the sound off should still follow the message from the screen.
- Open with a hook that is readable within a second: the question, promise or result in a few large words, landing on its key word. End on a payoff or clear call to action that repeats the key word.
- Scene length follows reading time: at least 3 seconds, usually 3.5–7 for a simple card and longer for diagrams. With narration, a scene lasts at least as long as its line plus about a second.
- Land each scene's key visual on the narration's key word, and hold the last frame for a moment before the next scene.
- Build each middle scene around one thing made for its beat, such as a chart, a diagram or a card, with the caption on a calm area below it, rather than text alone.
- Keep one visual system across scenes: the same palette, type, margins, light and motion style. Vary layout and the focal element, not identity. A small numbered kicker at the top can mark the steps.
- Change the light or the accent only at a change of chapter, and at most a few times in a video.
- Narration is the script's spoken words, written to be said aloud: short sentences, no lists of numbers, no parentheses.
