# Planning supporting visuals

- Every visual has a job tied to one script section: to explain, to prove, to orient (titles, lower thirds, chapter cards) or to brand (logo, end card). Drop anything without one.
- Prefer the creator's own library over new animations: real screenshots, logos and b-roll beat generic graphics.
- Take the palette, type and visual language from the brand colours, the workspace context and the reference images. Do not introduce colours or fonts the brand does not use. A strong palette is one main colour, one accent and two or three neutrals.
- Draw the picture from this idea. A visual that suits one topic, such as something growing, rarely suits the next; ask what this concept actually looks like.
- Explanations read best on light, calm surfaces. Choose dark when the brand or the subject calls for it, such as code, night or cinema, not by default.
- Compose each frame around one focal point, placed on a third of the frame unless symmetry is the point. Attention goes first to size, then contrast, then colour, then position. Leave empty space around the focal point; a crowded frame looks cheap.
- Information needs time to be read at normal speed: about 3 seconds at least, 5–7 seconds for a simple card, 8–14 seconds for a dense diagram. Quick accents on a beat can be 0.5–2 seconds because they only need to be recognised.
- Reveal one thing at a time. Two new elements appearing together split attention.
- Over speech, an animation should land its key frame on the word it illustrates and hold its last frame for at least a second.
- Background music is optional. One track usually carries a short video; change music only where the story changes chapter, and only when it fits the tone. Arrumo ducks it under speech and fades it out at the end.
- Sound effects only belong to something visible, such as a reveal, a click or a landing. A few deliberate ones sound designed; many sound cheap.
- Keep text short and large enough to read on a phone. In portrait frames keep it out of the bottom quarter, where platform controls and subtitles sit, and clear of the top edge and the right edge.
