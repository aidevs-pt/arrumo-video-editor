# Writing the script

- Start from the footage, not from an ideal script. The transcript is what the creator actually said; the script decides what survives and in which order.
- Name the video's shape before writing sections. Typical shapes: a hook, the problem, the answer, proof, a call to action (launches and demos); intro, setup, steps, pitfalls, recap (tutorials); question, answer, follow-up, repeated (interviews); claim, evidence, counterpoint, conclusion (explainers). Adapt or invent one when the material asks for it.
- The first seconds decide whether people stay. Open with the most concrete promise, result or surprise in the footage, not with a greeting.
- Mark verbal slips, wrong names, false starts and phrasings to avoid while reading the transcript, and say in the section text which take is cleaner when there are several.
- Never invent facts, numbers, quotes, prices or claims the footage and context do not support. Write estimates as estimates.
- Keep spontaneous reactions, demonstrations and personal experience: they make the video feel human. Plan to drop retrieval of tools, apologies to the camera and tangents.
- Give each section a realistic timing from the transcript. The sum should match the length the creator asked for or the profile's usual length; when it cannot, say what you would drop.
- Editing ideas should serve a specific moment: an animation that lands on the word it explains, a cut-away that shows the thing being talked about, a zoom on a detail. Avoid decorations without a job.
