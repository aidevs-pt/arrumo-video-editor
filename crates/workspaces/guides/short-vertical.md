# Short vertical videos

- The first second decides. Open on movement and on the promise of the payoff: the result, a surprising claim or a question. Put text on screen from the first frame, and never start with a logo, a greeting or a slow build.
- Shape: the hook, then beats that each raise the stakes, then the payoff, then an ending that loops cleanly back into the opening or points to the next video. Something should change every two or three seconds: a new line, a cut, a zoom, a number.
- Design for the sound being off. The message must be readable on screen; captions show a few words at a time in large type in the middle band, with the word being spoken marked. Keep them above the bottom quarter and away from the right edge, where the apps draw their buttons.
- One idea per screen in large, high-contrast type, a few words per line. If a frame needs more than two seconds of reading, split it.
- Countdowns and lists: a big number, the item's name and one picture per entry; count towards the best entry and hold it the longest; keep the other entries the same length.
- Message stories, told as a conversation in chat bubbles: one message at a time, a short typing pause before each reply and a longer one only before the twist, sent messages on one side and received ones on the other. Use a generic chat look in the brand's colours, never a real app's design.
- Names and roles: the name larger and the role smaller and muted, in the lower part of the frame but clear of captions and buttons, arriving and leaving within half a second and held for at least three seconds.
- Endings: hold the payoff for at least a second. A loop works when the last line leads naturally back into the first.
- Keep the video as short as the idea allows. Trim before adding.

Checks: the hook is readable within a second; something changes every few seconds; nothing important sits under the apps' buttons or captions; the ending loops or lands.
