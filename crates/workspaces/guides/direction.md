# Directing the look and motion

Decide how the video should feel before designing anything, say it in the plan, and hold every scene to it. A consistent system reads as confidence; a new curve or transition in every scene reads as noise.

- Find three words in the brief for how the video should feel, and one feeling to avoid, such as "warm, but not sugary" or "confident, but not loud". When the brief gives none, choose them from the audience and the brand, and say which you chose.
- Turn those words into a tone and keep it for the whole video. Some common tones, to adapt rather than copy:
  - Reassuring, for health, care, money or support: unhurried arrivals of about half a second or more, generous pauses between beats, no overshoot, gentle fades.
  - Confident, for business, software and services: arrivals of about a third of a second that stop cleanly, short travel, plain cuts with the odd quiet fade.
  - Playful, for families, hobbies and communities: a little spring when things land, loose timing, colour doing more of the work, pushes between scenes.
  - Urgent, for launches, sport, sales and countdowns: arrivals of about a fifth of a second, big jumps in size, a spring on the key hits only, hard cuts on the beat.
- Then fix the rules: one easing feel for arrivals and one for exits, one base duration with the others as multiples of it (half for small details, double for the hero move), one small set of transitions, one stagger rhythm, a limit on how far and how much things move, and a minimum stillness after each move.
- Read the brand into the motion as well as the colours: a luxury brand moves less and holds longer, a precise one keeps timing even and lines straight, a loud one uses heavy type and big contrasts, a warm one rounds its corners and its movements.
- Vague notes need a concrete reading before you act on them, whether they are in a brief or a review. "Cleaner" usually means fewer elements and colours with more space around them; "more energy" means shorter holds, faster arrivals and bolder colour; "too much going on" means fewer things moving at once; "looks cheap" usually comes from the type, the spacing, the easing or the colour. For any other vague word, find the moment it points at and the quality it is about. Choose the reading that fits that moment and name it in the summary.
- Restraint is part of direction. Keep backgrounds and most text still, never animate text while it is being read, keep overshoot away from serious and financial content, and allow one standout moment per video, since two compete and neither is remembered.
