# Directing the look and motion

Decide how the video should feel before designing anything, say it in the plan, and hold every scene to it. A consistent system reads as confidence; a new curve or transition in every scene reads as noise.

- Find three words in the brief for how the video should feel, and one feeling to avoid, such as "warm, but not sugary" or "confident, but not loud". When the brief gives none, choose them from the audience and the brand, and say which you chose.
- Place the video on two scales, calm to lively and soft to crisp, and stay with one of the four combinations:
  - Calm and soft (wellness, editorial, luxury): long, gentle arrivals of about 0.5–0.8 s, wide staggers, no overshoot, a pause of at least half a second after every beat, fades between scenes.
  - Calm and crisp (finance, business software, premium technology): decisive arrivals of about 0.3–0.5 s, small movements, almost no overshoot, cuts and quiet fades.
  - Lively and soft (lifestyle, children, community): springy arrivals with a little overshoot, looser timing, pushes between scenes.
  - Lively and crisp (sport, launches, games): fast arrivals of about 0.15–0.3 s, big changes of scale, overshoot on the hits, hard cuts.
- Then fix the rules: one easing feel for arrivals and one for exits, one base duration with the others as multiples of it (half for small details, double for the hero move), one small set of transitions, one stagger rhythm, a limit on how far and how much things move, and a minimum stillness after each move.
- Brand words become motion. Premium: slow, spacious, restrained, no bounce. Trustworthy: even timing and small, controlled moves. Bold: heavy type, strong contrast, large scale. Friendly: a slight spring and loose timing. Technical: crisp geometry and tight, layered staggers.
- Translate vague words before acting on them, in a brief or a review note. Clean: fewer elements, more space, fewer colours, slower easing. Pop or punchy: stronger colour, shorter holds, bigger changes of scale, more snap. Premium: slower, more space, a quieter palette, refined type. Dynamic: camera movement, faster cuts, more continuous motion. Simple: a single idea in each scene, no more than two things moving, one transition. Too busy: fewer things at once, a slower pace, fewer colours. Cheap: usually the type, spacing, easing or colour. Off: find the moment it points at and whether it is the pace, colour, type or message. Choose the reading that fits that moment and name it in the summary.
- Restraint is part of direction. Keep backgrounds and most text still, never animate text while it is being read, keep overshoot away from serious and financial content, and allow one standout moment per video, since two compete and neither is remembered.
