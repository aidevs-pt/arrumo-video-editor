# Talking heads, interviews and podcasts

- The speaker's words and delivery are the film. For each sentence, choose what is on screen: the speaker alone, the speaker with a graphic beside them, or a full-frame animation with their voice continuing underneath.
- Show the speaker alone for the opening line, personal opinions and stories, emotional moments, the call to action and the close. Viewers should meet the person first and leave with them.
- Add a graphic beside the speaker when a sentence contains something the viewer would gain from seeing: a key phrase, a figure that counts up, a list that builds item by item. Keep it towards a side, the top or the lower third and never over the face, show one idea at a time, and remove it once the sentence has passed. Use a few of the speaker's own words, not the whole sentence.
- Cover the speaker with a full-frame animation when a picture explains it better than a face: figures and comparisons, a sequence of steps, a mechanism, a product, or a list longer than two items. A cover usually lasts three to eight seconds. Come back to the speaker between covers so the viewer keeps the person in mind.
- Don't leave the speaker alone for much longer than eight seconds without a change, and don't change something on every line either.
- Time each element to the words. Structure the eye needs, such as axes, a card or an outline, can arrive a few frames before its word; a new figure must be readable on the word that says it; a punchline or an answer waits for the word that reveals it. Never move the audio to fit a graphic.
- Use only the figures, names and quotes the speaker says or the creator supplied.
- Interviews: cut on the answer rather than the question unless the question carries the story, name each person with a lower third the first time they appear, and keep reactions that land.
- Podcast clips for social: choose a moment that stands on its own, show who is speaking, keep captions large in the middle band, and when there is no picture, give the audio a speaker card with a moving waveform.
- Long videos: mark each chapter with a short title card in the same design every time.

Checks: the speaker opens and closes the video; no graphic touches the face; every figure lands on its word; each cover explains its line rather than decorating it.
